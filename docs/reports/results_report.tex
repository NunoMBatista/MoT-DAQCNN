\section{Overview}
This document provides a factual record of the experimental methodologies and results for the MoT-DAQCNN project on the BreastMNIST dataset. The primary objective is to quantify the performance of Analog-Digital Quantum Convolutional Neural Networks using hardware-native Rydberg atom interactions compared to various classical benchmarks.

\section{Study 1: End-to-End Performance Benchmarking}
This study evaluates the final classification performance of the full hybrid pipeline on BreastMNIST. Hyperparameters for each configuration were determined via 500--1,500 Optuna TPE trials; the best configuration was then retrained across 10--11 independent seeds to estimate mean~$\pm$~std. Results marked with~$\dagger$ are from trial seeds (not held-out validation seeds) and lack a std estimate; entries marked \emph{(running)} are still completing their HP search and report the current best trial mean.

Two encoding modes are compared throughout: \textbf{digital}, where each pixel is encoded via an $R_Y$ gate before Hamiltonian evolution, and \textbf{analog}, where pixel values are passed directly as the local detuning parameters $\delta_i$ of the Rydberg Hamiltonian, making the data drive the evolution itself.

\paragraph{Notes and caveats.}
All~$\dagger$ values report the best trial found by Optuna evaluated over 3--11 trial seeds; these differ from the held-out validation mean~$\pm$~std entries in that the same seeds were available to the optimiser. The most notable corrections relative to earlier snapshots: the Digital~ZZ 1-kernel entry previously showed~$0.852/0.864$ (a mid-search snapshot); the validated mean over 10 held-out seeds is~$0.876 \pm 0.010 / 0.904 \pm 0.008$. The best single-number AUC across all finished runs is~$0.922$ for the Analog~ZZ 1-kernel (star topology, 872 Optuna trials, 11 seeds per trial), with Digital~ZZ 4-kernel close behind at~$0.915$ (1,500 trials, 3 seeds per trial); both lack held-out validation and should be treated as upper-bound estimates.
The classical baseline~$\dagger$ values (Random: $0.899$; Trainable: $0.886$) are likewise from trial seeds over 1,000 Optuna trials and may overestimate held-out performance by~${\sim}1$--$2$\,pp relative to validated quantum entries. The TS-MoE HP search (not shown) returned an unusually low AUC of~$0.831$, which may reflect a poorly converged routing phase rather than an architectural ceiling; it requires a separate investigation before inclusion.
Two entries remain actively running: Vanilla~CNN and DAQCNN+ZZ (fixed vertical); their final validated mean~$\pm$~std will replace the preliminary values once complete.

\section{Study 2: Feature Probing Ablation (Linear Probing)}
This study isolates the representational power of the feature extraction layers. Features are extracted at two bottleneck points: ``After Kernel'' (immediately following the initial convolution/quantum layer) and ``Pre-Dense'' (following the complete classical processing head but before the final linear layer).

For each feature set, two neutral arbiters---a Random Forest (RF) with 100 estimators and a maximum depth of 10, and a Support Vector Machine (SVM) with an RBF kernel---are trained to classify the data. This evaluates the intrinsic separability and interpretability of the features generated by each architecture.

Results are reported as mean~$\pm$~std across 5 independent seeds (42, 1, 2, 3, 4). The quantum kernel features are \emph{task-agnostic}: the Rydberg Hamiltonian evolution is fixed and untrained; only the classical head is optimised per seed. Classical random filters are fixed at initialisation; classical trainable filters are optimised end-to-end. Bold marks the best value per column within each horizontal block.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
A first reading of the \emph{After Kernel} rows is sobering: the raw $\langle Z \rangle$ and $\langle ZZ \rangle$ outputs of the quantum layer ($3{,}645$ or $14{,}580$ dimensions for 1- and 4-kernel respectively) are essentially indistinguishable from a random classical projection and only marginally better than raw pixels. For example, the 1-kernel quantum AUC at this stage is $0.839 \pm 0.007$ (RF) versus $0.839$ for random classical projections and $0.842$ for raw pixels. This is not a sign that the quantum features carry no information; it is a consequence of \emph{dimensionality}: with roughly 546 training samples and up to 14,580 raw features, both RF and SVM are operating far above the sample-limited regime and their performance saturates regardless of feature source.

The picture changes sharply at the \emph{Pre-Dense} level, after the classical CNN head (Conv-BN-ReLU $\rightarrow$ MaxPool $\rightarrow$ Conv-ReLU) has compressed and spatially aggregated the kernel outputs into 576-dimensional global feature vectors. Here the quantum features yield RF AUC of $0.911 \pm 0.007$ and SVM AUC of $0.907 \pm 0.009$ for the 1-kernel case, compared to $0.880/0.889$ and $0.874/0.884$ for random and trainable classical filters respectively. The same hierarchy holds for 4 kernels. Critically, the quantum features are never trained on the task; their edge at the Pre-Dense level therefore cannot be attributed to task-specific optimisation. Instead, it suggests that the entanglement structure captured by the $\langle ZZ \rangle$ correlators---which encode genuine multi-partite correlations arising from the Rydberg Van der Waals interaction and are inaccessible to single-qubit measurements alone---provides a richer geometric basis that the classical aggregation head can exploit more effectively than it can exploit either random projections or learned classical filters.

Two caveats are worth stating clearly. First, the accuracy advantage of quantum features is modest and within the variance of classical alternatives; the consistent advantage is in AUC, which measures discriminative power across all decision thresholds and is more informative for the class-imbalanced BreastMNIST task. Second, this experiment evaluates a single fixed topology per kernel count; topology diversity (as in the TS-MoE pipeline) is expected to widen the gap further.
